📖 DNA: The Instruction Book for Life

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a super-tiny molecule with instructions for growing, living and having families. Every living thing uses it - you, a puppy, a mango tree and even tiny germs.

Think of DNA like a recipe book in every cell. Recipes tell your body how to grow taller, heal a cut, digest rice, and make your heart beat.

🪜 The Twisted Ladder

DNA looks like a twisted ladder. Scientists call it a double helix. The sides are called the backbone. They use sugar called deoxyribose and phosphate, taking turns like beads.

The steps are base pairs. Four letters only: A, T, C, G. A holds T, C holds G, with a gentle hug. One base + sugar + phosphate = a nucleotide, the building block.

Remember: A-T hold hands! C-G hold hands! If one side says A-T-C, the other says T-A-G.

🏠 Where Is DNA Found?

Most DNA lives in the cell nucleus. It is packed into bundles called chromosomes. People have 46, half from each parent.

A little DNA lives in mitochondria, the powerhouses that turn food into energy. All DNA together is your genome - about 3 billion steps, 99% the same in everyone!

⚙️ How DNA Works

Letter order spells genes. Genes build proteins, tiny workers for muscles, blood and eyes. DNA passes by heredity, so families look alike.

DNA can copy by replication. The ladder unzips, each side picks matches, and two exact books are made for new cells!

Did you know? DNA from one cell stretched out is about 2 metres long! It folds to fit in a dot you cannot see!

✉️ What Is RNA? The Helpful Messenger!

RNA (ribonucleic acid) is DNA's little helper. If DNA is the big recipe book that stays safe in the library, RNA is the small note card that copies one recipe and carries it to the kitchen. The cell reads the card to build a protein.

RNA looks like DNA but with three big differences kids can remember. One, RNA is usually one single line, not a ladder with two sides. Two, RNA uses the letter U instead of T. So A holds U, and C still holds G. Three, its sugar is a little different, called ribose, which makes RNA good for short trips outside the nucleus.

Remember: DNA = big book stays in library. RNA = small note goes to kitchen. DNA uses A-T, RNA uses A-U!

🎬 Watch: DNA Makes RNA!

Watch the blue DNA ladder unzip! A pink RNA note is being built below. Orange A letters fly down to match U, and green C letters fly to match G. This job is called transcription - copying one recipe!

DNA says A, RNA writes U! DNA says C, RNA writes G! The pink note then floats out to build a protein. Tap the ladder game below to try matching!
